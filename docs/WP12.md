# WP12 — Explain this record, and Life and times

From [Update 1](updates/UPDATE-1_Build_Specification.pdf) (ADR-048).

## What was built (11 Nov 2026)

- **Explain this record** (`/explain`, "Explain" in the top bar, and "Explain this record" on any Archive file with text). Four layers, kept apart:
  1. **What it says**: the record's wording, unchanged, with its year and place.
  2. **What the words mean**: checked explanations of its terms, institutions and record types, for the right period and place. A record from before an institution existed says so (a 1790 "workhouse" was a parish one, not a union workhouse) and points to the earlier records instead.
  3. **What it may mean for them**: careful, general readings, labelled as what was common, never what happened to this person ("people went into workhouses for many reasons; this record alone doesn't say why").
  4. **Where to find out more**: records to look for, where they're kept, and the county record office for the place.
  Workhouse and poor relief records carry a note that they can be hard to read and that descendants decide what's shared.
- **Knowledge base**: the checked glossary gained period, area, "may mean" and "where to look" fields. Starter Poor Law entries come from The National Archives' guides (workhouse, pauper, admitted and discharged, quarter sessions), alongside the earlier union, guardians, settlement and overseer entries.
- **Offered explanations**: anyone can offer an explanation with a source and how they'd like to be credited. It's shown, credited, once an editor marks it reviewed in the admin.
- **Life and times** on each life story, with a year slider across their recorded life:
  - Money: how prices compared with now (ONS long-run Retail Prices Index, from 1800), with the caution that it's an average price comparison, not wages.
  - Weather: the year in central England (Met Office HadCET, from 1659), its coldest and warmest months, and whether it was one of the coldest or warmest on record; with the caution that it's a region, not their town.
  - What was happening: wars, laws and census nights (the Lifeline's checked list).
  - How people described it: a pointer to diaries and oral histories, which need permission to show.
- **Assistant**: `explain_record` and `life_and_times` tools, with citations.
- Data files are kept in `genealogy/data` (OGL) and refreshed with `python manage.py refresh_context_data`.
