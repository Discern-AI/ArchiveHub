# WP14 — Life Report and culture links

From [Update 1](updates/UPDATE-1_Build_Specification.pdf) (ADR-048).

## What was built (11 Nov 2026)

- **Culture items**, each with its rights: kind, creator, when it was made, the places and subjects it shows, the holder's link, licence, credit line and the date its rights were checked. Rights decide everything: an image is shown only when they're open, and otherwise the item is linked. Curators approve items in the admin; only approved ones are used.
- **Starter set, checked at the source today**:
  - Cleveland Museum of Art (each object's own CC0 flag): John Sell Cotman, *A Norfolk Plough* (1814); Peter Henry Emerson, *Rowing Home the Schoof-Stuff* (1886, from *Life and Landscape on the Norfolk Broads*); Charles Angrand, *End of the Harvest* (French, c. 1892–1905), labelled as a comparison from abroad.
  - The Met (each object's own public-domain flag): Thomas Rowlandson, *Haymakers* (1787); an anonymous British *Harvest Scene* (18th century).
  - Halliwell's *The Nursery Rhymes of England* (fifth edition, Frederick Warne, 1886) via Project Gutenberg: "He that would thrive", "In July, some reap rye", and lines from an alphabet rhyme of trades.
- **Matching** uses only documented details: the work their recorded occupation involves, when they lived, and whether the item is from their country. Never class assumptions. Each match gives its reasons ("It shows haymaking, the kind of work recorded for Thomas (agricultural labourer). It was made in 1787, about 38 years before…"). Rhymes say their printing date isn't when they were made up. Reports mix pictures and words.
- **Life Report** ("Write their Life Report" on the life story): title, at a glance, their life in order, the world around them (Life and times for a year of their adult life), how artists and writers saw their world (two items short, three long, each labelled "This shows their world, not them", with the note that artists often made country life look more picturesque than it was), and sources and gaps.
  - Short or long; edit any text or leave an item out; save versions; print or save as PDF (checked in Chrome).
  - Written from the records, without AI, so it's free; the page says so.
  - Saving re-reads every culture item from the approved record, so an image or credit can't be swapped and nothing unapproved can be added.
  - Not written for anyone who may be living.
- **FictionalPlace** model for links like Hardy's Wessex names. None are loaded: the links in Update 1 come from a secondary source and need checking against Hardy scholarship first.
- The Met retired its old search on 1 October 2026; ArchiveHub's Met connector already uses the new v1.1 search.
